## Task 2 — Write an Email ⏱ 7 分鐘

**Situation**

You have been admitted to a graduate computer science program. You want to register for **CS 6200: Introduction to Operating Systems** this fall, but the online registration system shows that the course is full. You decide to write to the instructor.

**Write an email to Professor Alan Whitfield.** Your email must:

1. Introduce yourself and clearly state which course you are writing about
2. Explain **why** you want to take this specific course this fall (give a concrete reason)
3. Ask whether there is a waitlist or whether he can approve an exception

**Length**: about 80–120 words. Include a greeting and a closing.

## Task 3 — Writing for an Academic Discussion ⏱ 10 分鐘

Your professor is teaching a class on education and technology. Write a post responding to the professor's question. In your response you should:

* State and support your own opinion
* **Respond to at least one classmate by name** (agree, disagree, or add something they missed)

**Length**: at least 100 words.

<br>

> **Professor Nguyen**
>
> Many universities are now deciding how to handle AI coding assistants. Some allow students to use them freely on programming assignments; others ban them completely. Both sides argue that they are protecting the quality of education.
>
> My question for you this week: **should universities allow students to use AI tools when completing their coursework?** Explain your position and give reasons.

> **Marta**
>
> I think universities should allow them. When calculators appeared, mathematics teachers panicked too, but nobody today argues that engineers should compute square roots by hand. AI assistants are simply the next tool. Every company my friends work for expects new hires to use them productively, so a university that bans them is preparing students for a workplace that no longer exists.

> **Kwame**
>
> I disagree with Marta. The difficulty of writing code yourself is not an obstacle to learning — it *is* the learning. When a student asks an AI to produce a function, they receive the answer without ever building the mental model that would let them debug it later. A calculator does not pretend to understand arithmetic on your behalf; these tools do.
